# EDITORIAL CHARTER
### The reader comes first. Everything else is subordinate to that.

JW, 2026-09-11. Issued against the 75 comments on `Medicaid_Dollars_National_DRAFT.pdf`.

**This charter sits above `EDITORIAL_STANDING_NOTES.md`.** Where a standing note
conflicts with a rule here, this document wins and the note is marked superseded in
place rather than deleted. The charter applies to the national edition, every state
edition, and anything else this project publishes.

---

## C-00 · Who the reader is, and what we owe them

The reader is a time-starved executive somewhere in the Medicaid ecosystem. They want
the insight fast, with no windup.

Three obligations follow, and every other rule in this charter is downstream of them.

**The paper must never make the reader feel stupid, overwhelmed, or insufficiently
expert to follow what we have done.**

**The paper must leave the reader better equipped to handle the change HR-1 represents
to their organisation.** Not better informed about our methods. Better equipped.

**The paper must not perform its own rigour.** Any sentence whose real content is "look
how careful we were" is cut. The authors do not need to prove the work is sound, and
the reader will decide for themselves. This applies to the Author drafting it as much
as to the firm publishing it.

---

## C-01 · The governing idea

**C-01.1 · Extract the governing idea before writing anything.** Find the one sentence a
reader should still be able to repeat tomorrow. Lead with it at full weight, with nothing
competing on the same page. If the draft has two candidate sentences, the paper has two
papers in it and one of them is in the wrong document.

**C-01.2 · Frame the contrast before any data appears.** State the what-is against the
what-could-be in plain language first. The numbers prove the story. They are not the
story, and a paper that opens on figures has skipped the step that makes the figures
mean something.

**C-01.3 · Human stakes before mechanism.** Open with who is affected and what changes
for them. Explain how the ecosystem works afterwards, and only as much as the stakes
require.

**C-01.4 · Credit the model, not the output.** Before describing a diagram, a chart or a
number as the achievement, check whether it is the rendering of something larger: a
model, a system, a body of work. Name that thing first. The diagrams in this project are
the visible surface of a conserved ledger of the Medicaid funding lifecycle. The ledger
is the achievement and the diagram is how it is read.

*C-01 in full, plus C-04.7, C-06.5 and C-09.6, come from the firm's editorial expert, 2026-09-11.*

## C-02 · Length

A paper runs at 35 to 50 percent of the length it would reach if written without this
constraint. The cut comes out of prose. **Tables and figures are not cut to make
length**; they are what distinguishes the work.

The national edition target is 4,000 to 5,500 words of body against the 11,400 it
carried at first draft.

## C-03 · Structure

**C-04.1 · Every introduction summarises both views and the difference between them.**
An intro that describes only the as-is view is incomplete. Applies to every white paper
this project publishes, not only this one. (#14)

**C-04.2 · Name the two views plainly and early.** The first is an as-is, or as-was,
view. The second is a projected to-be view. Use those words. (#9, #10)

**C-04.3 · No cross-references to other sections at a section opening.** Nobody outside
the firm cares about our outline. A section begins with its subject. (#37, #38)

**C-04.4 · Section-opening throat-clearing is cut.** If the first sentence explains what
the section is about to do rather than doing it, delete it.

---

## C-04 · Sentences

**C-04.1 · Break up run-on sentences.** (#3)

**C-04.2 · No subjectless fragments.** "Nine hundred billion over ten years. A tenth of
the program." Both are fragments and both go. Every sentence carries a subject and a
verb. (#4)

**C-04.3 · Cut empty connective phrases.** "as it stood", "in the ordinary sense", "it
is worth stating that". If removing the phrase costs no meaning, remove it. (#7)

**C-04.4 · No rhetorical flourishes.** Banned outright: "Hold that distinction." "That
is the headline." "Two observations readers consistently find counterintuitive." "It is
worth one sentence and no more." Anything that stage-manages the reader's attention
instead of giving them the point. (#41, #58, #61)

**C-04.5 · Do not address the reader in the second person inside a section written for
them.** A section for providers does not say "your payer mix". The provider is present
when they read it. Write about the situation, not at the person. (#72)

**C-04.6 · Never explain our editorial rules to the reader.** No sentences about how we
treat terms of art, how we handle figures, or what we decided to include. Those belong
in technical documentation or a footnote. (#24, #31)

**C-04.7 · The Brent Pass.** Run it over every draft before it leaves.

- Cut throat-clearing. If the sentence warms up to a point, start at the point.
- Short declaratives.
- No hedging. Certainty belongs in the prose; uncertainty belongs in the quarantine
  (C-06.5) and in the footnotes (C-06.1).
- One idea per unit. A paragraph carrying two ideas is two paragraphs.
- If it would not survive being said across a table by colleagues, rewrite it.

*From the firm's editorial expert, 2026-09-11.*

## C-05 · Stating numbers

**C-05.1 · State the positive figure first, then the decrement.** Not "$13.73 did not
become a health service." Rather "$86.27 paid a claim for a health service, which means
$13.73 did not." The reader meets what the system delivered before they meet what it
lost. (#13, #36, #59)

**C-05.2 · Where a table column is a sum, make it visually a sum.** Rule above it, bold
it, or both. (#69)

**C-05.3 · Order tables by what the reader is looking for**, which is usually largest
effect to smallest, not source order. (#70)

**C-05.4 · Label estimated columns as estimated.** A rate column that carries a modelled
figure says so in its header. (#70)

---

## C-06 · Gaps, limits and methodology

**C-06.1 · A limit gets a rigorous footnote and nothing in the body.** Superseding the
earlier practice of declaring gaps in the body text. Not a clause, not a sentence, not a
parenthetical. The footnote carries the full statement of what is absent, why, and what
we are doing about it, and it is rigorous rather than brief. The body text carries the
finding. (#31, #74, and JW 2026-09-11)

This changes nothing about the artifact. Declarations printed on a diagram stay on the
diagram.

**C-06.2 · Say "this is not published, and we are pursuing it."** Not "we cannot tell
you", which sounds like a failing, and not "we have not done the research", which is
inaccurate where the underlying data is genuinely unpublished. (#30, and JW ruling)

**C-06.3 · State an assumption plainly instead of justifying it.** One sentence naming
the assumption. No paragraph explaining why it was reasonable. (#54, #57, #62, #63, #64)

**C-06.4 · Explained, not defended.** A modelling choice is explained to the reader. It
is not defended against an imagined critic. (#44)

**C-06.5 · Methodology quarantine. The footnotes are the quarantine.**

State the measured-against-modelled boundary once, in the body, at the first place it
matters. After that it is quarantined: every further statement about what is measured,
what is modelled and what is absent goes into a footnote.

The body never relitigates trust. A reader who has been told once where the boundary sits
does not need reminding at each figure, and a paper that reminds them reads as though it
expects not to be believed.

This supersedes C-06.1 as written. C-06.1 said a limit gets a rigorous footnote and
nothing in the body. C-06.5 adds the exception: the first statement of the boundary earns
its place in the body, and only the first.

*From the firm's editorial expert, 2026-09-11.*

## C-07 · Vocabulary

**C-07.1 · Say "ecosystem", not "system", for the whole of Medicaid.** Money leaves the
ecosystem. Readers sit somewhere in the ecosystem. (#16, #17)

**C-07.2 · Never say "the Medicaid dollar" in body prose.** The title "$100 Medicaid
Dollars" is the product name and stands. The singular abstraction does not. Say what
actually moves: funding, payments, claims, revenue. (#17)

*Standing scan.* The phrase is in the codebase and the decision record, not only in the
prose, which is why it keeps reappearing in drafts. At the start of any session that
pulls fresh from the repo, grep for it and report the count before drafting. At issue
on 2026-09-11: 24 instances across 15 files, including `sankey.py`, `tobe2030.py`,
`financing.py`, `ledger_dc.py`, `build_xlsx.py` and `ARCHITECTURE.md`.

**C-07.3 · Say "phases", not "columns", in body prose.** The eight columns of the
diagram represent phases of the funding lifecycle. The diagram keeps its column
headers; the prose talks about phases. (#8, #39, and JW ruling)

**C-07.4 · Say "where in the lifecycle", not "where in the system".** (#18)

**C-07.5 · Gloss the statute once, early, twice over.** The law is cited as P.L. 119-21.
Medicaid practitioners say HR-1. Bind the two on the cover and again at first use in the
introduction, then let the public law number carry the rest. (Q-01)

**C-07.6 · "Lost" is under review.** The balance line prints it and the prose follows it.
It is the wrong word for money that pays people to do necessary work, and the reader
doing that work should not be told their function is a loss. Do not change it yet. A
replacement has to work at all four anchors, including the one that includes fraud.
(#33, and JW ruling)

## C-08 · The reader's own position

**C-08.1 · A reader must be able to find their own place in the ecosystem** and
understand how their world will change. That is the job of the paired diagrams and it
should be said in those terms. (#11, #12)

**C-08.2 · The national view is a baseline for comparison, not a description of anyone's
local conditions.** Managed care and fee-for-service splits vary enormously by state.
Say so once, plainly, where the lanes are introduced. (#42)

**C-08.3 · Write for the worry, not for the ledger.** A section about providers is about
business impact. The numbers support it; they are not the subject. (#68)

## C-09 · Figures and tables

**C-09.1 · Figures earn their place and are never cut for length.** (C-01)

**C-09.2 · Put the figure next to the passage it serves.** Provider bar graphs sit beside
the provider passages, not on a distant page. (#73, #75)

**C-09.3 · Pair the two balance lines.** The introduction carries the as-is and to-be
number lines stacked directly on top of each other, so the difference is read rather
than described. (#15)

**C-09.4 · Detail figures are crops of the master at native resolution**, never redrawn,
so a detail cannot disagree with the panel.

**C-09.5 · Long explanatory passages attached to a table belong in an endnote tied to
that table**, not in the body beside it. (#71)

**C-09.6 · One encoding per visual.** Before building any chart or diagram, ask whether
it asks the eye to do more than one job at once. If it does, build it in stages rather
than layering it. A figure that carries flow, magnitude, category and change
simultaneously is four figures, and the reader will read none of them.

This applies to new visuals. Where an existing panel already layers encodings, the remedy
is a staged crop in the paper rather than a redraw of the artifact.

*From the firm's editorial expert, 2026-09-11.*

## C-09A · The figure shows what the page discusses

**C-09A.1 · A reader expects the picture to correspond to the topic.** If a page names a
provision, a band or a node, a figure on that page shows it. A crop that omits the subject
under discussion sends the reader hunting through earlier pages, and they will assume they
have missed something rather than that we cropped badly.

**C-09A.2 · This is measured, not judged.** `figurefit.py` reads the label coordinates out
of the panel SVGs, the crop box each figure cuts out of `paper_figs/crops.json`, and the
figure captions that open on each page of the rendered PDF. It reports every subject named
in prose whose label falls outside every crop on that page. Run it with `whitespace.py` on
every render.

**C-09A.3 · The fix is usually the crop, not the prose.** Widening a crop by a hundred
units is cheaper than moving a paragraph, and the crops are generated rather than drawn, so
widening one is a line in `make_figs.py`.

*Written 2026-09-11 after JW found the provision discussed on a page appearing in no figure
on that page or the next. The first version of the detector passed the page, because the
caption's own words "Detail from Figure 5" counted the whole panel as present, and the whole
panel contains everything. It now counts a figure as present only where its caption opens on
the page.*

## C-10 · Page economy

**C-10.1 · A full-page diagram plate carries the diagram's own title as the page
heading.** The title is already written into `instances.py`. Use it verbatim rather than
inventing a heading for the page. (JW 2026-09-11)

**C-10.2 · No page ends on a near-empty column.** A page carrying two short blocks and
white space below is a layout failure, not a natural break. Pull the next page's opening
material up, and if the page still will not close, reduce a figure or tighten a table.
Prose is not cut to fix pagination; figures and tables are resized. (JW 2026-09-11)

**C-10.3 · A figure placed mid-section can strand a column.** In a two-column flow a
figure that cannot fit the remaining column height jumps to the next column and leaves
the space behind it empty. Place the figure at the head of its subsection so the prose
after it can fill both columns.

**C-10.4 · The cover subtitle stays plain.** It names the paper and the law. Any gloss,
binding or qualification goes in the deck beneath the cover figure, never in the
headline. (JW 2026-09-11)

## C-10A · The layout gate

**C-10A.0 · A layout detector measures both axes, and measures the frame rather than the
art.** `whitespace.py` originally measured only vertical dead space, so spanning a
portrait figure across the page passed clean. The first repair asked the image how wide it
was, which is the wrong object: a portrait crop centred in a full-width frame is narrow by
definition, so the test declared it a column figure and stayed silent. The working check
measures the caption rule, which is the frame, against the widest artwork sitting on it.

A detector that measures one axis is blind on the other. A detector pointed at the wrong
object agrees with itself.

**C-10A.1 · Whitespace is measured, not judged.** `whitespace.py` rasterises every page
of the rendered PDF and reports, per column, the dead space below the lowest inked pixel,
in text lines. A body page that strands more than six lines in either column fails. Cover
pages, full-page plates and the last page are exempt by design. Run it on every render,
the way `crossings.py` runs on every build.

The eye is not a detector. Judging layout by looking at a contact sheet is the same error
as judging ribbon crossings by looking at a panel, and it produced the same result: the
defect kept coming back because nothing caught it.

**C-10A.2 · Everything vertical is a multiple of the line height.** Figure heights,
margins, rules and box padding all snap to the body line. Remaining space is then always a
whole number of blank lines rather than a ragged remainder, and a paragraph either fits or
it does not.

**C-10A.3 · Figures float to the head of a column. Never mid-column.** A figure that
cannot fit the remaining column height jumps and strands the space behind it.

**C-10A.4 · A figure spans the page only if its artwork is wide enough to fill it.**
The test is the aspect ratio against the text width divided by the column width. On this
grid the text is 251mm and a column is 120mm, so a figure spans only at an aspect of
roughly 2.5:1 or wider. The paired balance lines at 3.8:1 span. The lanes crop at 3.4:1
spans. A portrait crop at 0.8:1 does not, and spanning one produces a small picture
marooned in the middle of a full-width frame with 150mm of white on either side.

*Written 2026-09-11 after doing exactly that to two figures.* C-10A.3 was applied as a
cure for vertical stranding without an aspect test, which traded a defect in one dimension
for a worse one in the other.

## Superseded standing notes

Marked in place in `EDITORIAL_STANDING_NOTES.md`, not deleted.

- **S-071, as applied to prose.** Absent data was declared in the body. It is now
  declared in a rigorous footnote. The artifact rule is unchanged.
- **S-002, in part.** The firm still takes a position. It no longer announces that it is
  taking one. "The firm's position, offered as mechanism rather than as prediction" and
  every construction like it is cut. (#66)
- **S-033, in part.** Plain-language labels still lead. Parenthetical section numbers at
  first use survive, but cross-references between sections do not. (C-02.3)

Unchanged and still in force: S-005 (no em-dashes), S-006 (no "not X, it's Y"), S-003
(non-partisan on motive, unflinching on consequence), S-058, S-073, and the conservation
and build-provenance rules, which are about whether a number is right rather than about
how it reads.

**2026-09-11 · Rules from the second redline (28 comments).**

- **C-09.7 · No headings over tables.** A table is introduced by the prose above it or it
  stands alone. Adding a heading to some tables and not others is worse than adding none.
- **C-09.8 · Never split a table across a column or page.** If it will not fit, inject
  prose between it and the figure above so the table starts higher, or shorten the prose.
  Splitting is not an option, and the one place it was allowed is now closed.
- **C-07.6 · American spelling.** There is no Medicaid in Britain. Spell-check against a
  US dictionary. Author biographies are quoted verbatim and are exempt.
- **C-09A.4 · A page that treats a diagram element as its subject shows that element.**
  The check counts a subject as present only where it appears in a heading or a bold
  lead-in, because a provision named in passing inside a paragraph is a cross-reference and
  a reader does not expect a picture for it. A page with a subject and no diagram at all is
  reported the same way as a page whose crop misses it.

**2026-09-14 · Working files stay out of the repo.**
Every commit this session needed a list of files to delete by hand, because drafts,
intermediate renders and superseded templates were written into the repo alongside the
work. That is the Author's habit, not a requirement.

From now: intermediate renders, superseded templates and comparison PDFs live in the
working directory and are never committed. The repo carries the live source, the live
render, one prior version as comparison, and the build and gate code. When a version is
retired, its predecessor's predecessor goes on the delete list rather than accumulating.

`DELETE OLD FILES.command` exists because the habit already created a backlog. It should
get shorter every commit, not longer. If it is growing, the rule above is being ignored.
