# Paper passages

Publication-voice prose, written as the finding lands rather than reconstructed at
drafting time. Each entry names the section it belongs to. No manuscript exists
yet; these are drop-ins for when it does.

House rules observed: no em-dashes (S-005), no "it's not X, it's Y" (S-006), plain
language leads and section numbers follow (S-033), future tense on the to-be
(D-56), the firm takes a position (S-002), non-partisan on motive and unflinching
on consequence (S-003).

## P-01 · Methodology section: what the $100 does and does not contain

**Placement:** Methodology, immediately after the unit is defined and before any
figure is read off the diagram. This has to come early, because a reader who does
not have it will look for inflation on the diagram and conclude it was missed.

> **What the hundred dollars is.**
>
> Both diagrams read in dollars per one hundred dollars of Medicaid spending. The
> FY2024 diagram divides a hundred dollars of actual FY2024 spending. The FY2030
> diagram divides a hundred dollars of what FY2030 spending would have been under
> the law as it stood before P.L. 119-21.
>
> That choice buys comparability and it costs scale, and both consequences matter
> to how the figures should be read.
>
> The comparability is the point. Because the same denominator convention governs
> both diagrams, every node sits in the same place and every band means the same
> thing. A hospital finance officer can put the two side by side and read the
> change at the hospital bar without adjusting for anything.
>
> The cost is that the hundred dollars is a unit rather than an amount of money.
> Medical prices will rise between 2024 and 2030, and Medicaid spending will rise
> with them. The Congressional Budget Office projects federal Medicaid outlays
> growing from $656 billion in FY2025 to $837 billion in FY2030 under prior law,
> roughly five percent a year, at a time when prior-law enrollment is close to
> flat. Almost all of that growth is price and intensity.
>
> None of it appears here. Dividing by the total removes anything that moves the
> numerator and the denominator together, and general price growth moves both. It
> is therefore absent from every column of both diagrams. It is not concealed in
> the federal band, and it has not been left out by oversight.
>
> The practical rule for reading these pages: **the diagrams answer how the
> Medicaid dollar is divided, and they do not answer what the dollar is worth.**
> Those are separate questions and this paper keeps them separate.[^N]

[^N]: The temptation to reinsert price growth as a deduction near the provider end
of the FY2030 diagram is real, and we tested it before rejecting it. A deduction of
that kind applies a price level to an index number, which is a mixed-basis
comparison and produces a figure that cannot be reconciled with either diagram. The
quantity that does belong to the margin question is a ratio rather than a level:
the gap between growth in Medicaid payment and growth in provider input costs. That
gap requires a provider input-cost measure such as a CMS market basket index set
against the CBO payment path. It cannot be constructed by differencing CBO's
five percent against the CMS Office of the Actuary's projection of 5.8 percent
per-enrollee Medicaid spending growth over 2025 through 2033, because both are
projections of spending rather than measures of cost, and differencing two
estimates of the same quantity produces an artifact of the estimators.

## P-02 · Provider section: the sentence that keeps the reader's trust

**Placement:** wherever the paper first speaks to health systems, nursing
facilities and physician practices about what FY2030 will feel like. Probably the
opening of the provider discussion.

> Providers reading this should expect the squeeze to arrive from two directions,
> and only one of them is in these diagrams.
>
> P.L. 119-21 will take $10.26 out of every hundred Medicaid dollars by FY2030,
> and health services delivered will fall by $8.48, from $86.27 to $77.79. That is
> the part we can size, lane by lane, from published estimates.
>
> The second direction is cost. What it takes to staff a shift, stock a pharmacy
> and run a building will keep rising, and Medicaid payment will not track it
> automatically in any state. That pressure is real, it is what will show up in
> the margin line, and it is not on these pages, for the reason set out in the
> methodology: the per-hundred-dollar frame removes it by construction.
>
> We are not treating that as a footnote. We are treating it as a second analysis
> that requires a different measure, and we would rather say so than publish a
> number built on the wrong basis.

## P-03 · Optional, for the executive summary

**Placement:** only if the summary discusses what the paper deliberately excludes.
Cut if the summary is tight.

> One thing this paper does not do. It does not estimate what rising costs will do
> to provider margins by 2030. That effect is real and it is probably larger than
> anything in the statute. It also requires a measure of provider input cost rather
> than a measure of spending, and the published projections do not supply one. We
> would rather leave a named gap than fill it with a figure that will not hold.

## Open drafting note

P-02 now says services delivered fall $8.48, from $86.27 to $77.79, which is a
figure straight off the tracker and is safe. An earlier draft of this passage
called that the provider-borne share of the $10.26, which it is not. The gap
between $10.26 and $8.48 is not one thing:

- **$1.27** blocked senior enrollment rule, which lands on dual eligibles as higher
  Medicare out-of-pocket cost and never reaches a Medicaid provider (EN-27).
- **$1.24** provider tax limits, federal match never drawn, which lands on state
  budgets and on the providers whose tax was leveraging that match (EN-25).
- Offset by the ordinary leakage that also shrinks, so the arithmetic does not
  reduce to a simple subtraction.

**Do not publish a "provider-borne share" figure until that decomposition is
clean.** It is the same open item as EN-21 and EN-25 and it is now blocking prose
as well as the diagram.
